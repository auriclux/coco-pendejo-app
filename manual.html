Tell Tío Clave

Before every other chapter, this one, because it is the fix for all the others.

In the board's 🧰 Shop menu there is an item: 🎤 Tell Tío Clave What You Think. Tap it, speak or type whatever is on your mind, and send. It goes straight to Tío Clave as an email, from the shop, in your words.

Use it for anything: something broken, something confusing, something the app did that was dumb, something you wish it did. If a chapter in this manual does not match what the app in front of you does, that is worth a message too: the manual gets fixed from what you send.

Nothing in this app is finished forever. That button is how it keeps getting better.

This is the road every job travels, on a good day with nothing strange on it. Ten steps. At each step: what happens, what you do, what the customer sees, and what Coco writes down. Steps marked COMING are built on this same road; the road does not change.

1. Customer contact. A car has a problem and its owner finds the shop, the way owners always have: a call, a visit, a name passed along. Nothing in the app replaces that conversation. The app enters at the moment the conversation turns into work. LIVE the human way.

2. Job acceptance. You look at the car, you talk it over, and you decide: this shop takes this job. That decision is the Commit. In the app you tap New Job, name the customer and the car, name the job, and choose: start now, or stage it. Staging is for a car that is here but waiting its turn; you write the agreed condition in plain words (“when the truck is done”) and the customer sees exactly that as the status. Coco creates the job and makes the customer's invitation link. You send the link by text or email from your own phone. LIVE

3. Deposit. A job that is committed carries a deposit. You enter the amount at the Commit, or after, and the customer never sees it; it is shop record. Money amounts in this app are held sealed, encrypted, with the key in your hands: your phone, and the flash drive backup you keep. Lose both and the amounts are gone; the backup is not a detail. Capture LIVE Sealed store COMING No real money goes in until it lands.

4. Work order prep. The job becomes a list of tasks on the board, in the order the work runs. Today you build the list. Coming: Coco drafts the list from the job's name and your past jobs, and you prune it; hers is a draft, yours is the order. LIVE by hand Drafts COMING

5. Task management. Two different acts live here, and the whole app hangs on the difference. A check says the work is done, claimed by whoever did it, worker or you, with a name on it. A Sign Off is yours alone: you looked, it is so, and the sign-off publishes the car's whole Current Status to the customer in one motion. The customer never sees the board; they see the last thing you signed. A signed task that has to reopen says so, with its date, until you sign it again. LIVE

6. Part purchase. A task needs a part, and there are two ways it gets bought. The first: from the part you tap Ask Customer to Buy and paste the seller's link; add your judgment if you have some. The customer pays the seller directly and has it shipped to the shop; the money never passes through your hands or the app. The customer answers Bought it, Not now, or Call me, and sends a photo of the receipt when they have bought. The second way: the shop buys it. Then the part's record says so, with what the shop paid (sealed, like all money), and the part becomes a line on the job's invoice. A part the customer bought is listed on the record too, marked customer-supplied, and is never a charge.

The part walks its own road: Needed, Asked, Ordered, Arrived; Arrived is marked at the shop, and every part not yet arrived sits on the pending list, grouped by car. One record, one road, whoever buys: if a part is already on order and somebody records buying it again, from either side, the app does not quietly nod. It flags the duplicate for you to sort out, because two of a part is a real thing that happens in real shops. Ask flow LIVE Receipt photo + duplicate flag COMING

7. Job completion. The last task signs off and the job is done as work. You tap Complete Job. The customer's page closes the job with its date and keeps the whole Story readable; the record goes read-only, and if the customer has a staged car waiting, the app offers to start it. LIVE

8. Invoice prep. The job's accounting, kept in the app from the start, becomes the invoice: labor from the hours record, parts at what the record says, the deposit credited, the balance plain. You review it before it exists for anyone else; nothing reaches the customer that you have not seen. COMING with the accounting build.

9. Final payment. Most days this step and the next are the same moment: the customer arrives with the payment in hand and leaves with the car. The app records the payment against the job as it happens, so the ledger and the drawer agree. COMING with the accounting build.

10. Delivery. The customer collects the car. Delivery is your call, not the balance's: sometimes you release a car on a partial payment and credit, because the workspace is worth more than the wait. The app is built for that. The job closes; the account does not have to. What is still owed becomes the customer's tab, kept on their account across jobs, and later payments knock it down until it reaches zero. Their page keeps the finished job and its Story as their record of the work, and the next job for that car, whenever it comes, opens as a new chapter on the same car, under the same invitation. Nothing about the old job rewrites itself. Job side LIVE Tab COMING

That is the whole road. Every other chapter in this book is one of these steps, opened up.
