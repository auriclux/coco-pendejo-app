Before anything else

Put Coco on your phone

The app works best as an app. Put Coco's face on your home screen and she opens full screen, like any app on the phone, with no browser bars around her.

iPhone: open your board link in Safari. Tap the Share button, the square with the arrow pointing up. Scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen, then Add.

Android: open your board link in Chrome. Tap the ⋮ menu, then Add to Home screen (some phones call it Install app), and confirm.

Do it from your own board page, the link with your key in the address, so the icon opens your shop. Your customers can do the same from their car page: their icon opens their car.

Start here

Tell Tío Clave

Before every other chapter, this one, because it is the fix for all the others.

In the board's 🧰 Shop menu there is an item: 🎤 Tell Tío Clave What You Think. Tap it, speak or type whatever is on your mind, and send. It goes straight to Tío Clave as an email, from the shop, in your words.

Use it for anything: something broken, something confusing, something the app did that was dumb, something you wish it did. If a chapter in this manual does not match what the app in front of you does, that is worth a message too: the manual gets fixed from what you send.

Nothing in this app is finished forever. That button is how it keeps getting better.

This is the road every job travels, on a good day with nothing strange on it. Ten steps. At each step: what happens, what you do, what the customer sees, and what Coco writes down. Steps marked COMING are built on this same road; the road does not change.

1. Customer contact. A car has a problem and its owner finds the shop, the way owners always have: a call, a visit, a name passed along. Nothing in the app replaces that conversation. The app enters at the moment the conversation turns into work. LIVE the human way.

2. Job acceptance. You look at the car, you talk it over, and you decide: this shop takes this job. That decision is the Commit. In the app you tap New Job, name the customer and the car, name the job, and choose: start now, or stage it. Staging is for a car that is here but waiting its turn; you write the agreed condition in plain words (“when the truck is done”) and the customer sees exactly that as the status. Coco creates the job and makes the customer's invitation link. You send the link by text or email from your own phone. LIVE

3. Deposit. A job that is committed carries a deposit. You enter the amount at the Commit, or after, and the customer never sees it; it is shop record. Money amounts in this app are held sealed, encrypted, with the key in your hands: your phone, and the flash drive backup you keep. Lose both and the amounts are gone; the backup is not a detail. Capture LIVE Sealed store COMING No real money goes in until it lands.

4. Work order prep. The job becomes a list of tasks on the board, in the order the work runs. Today you build the list. Coming: Coco drafts the list from the job's name and your past jobs, and you prune it; hers is a draft, yours is the order. LIVE by hand Drafts COMING

5. Task management. The board is the record now. It does not live on one phone: every device signed in with your shop key shows the same board, and every change is saved to the record the moment it is made. With no signal there are no saves; the board shows the last state it loaded and waits. Two different acts live here, and the whole app hangs on the difference. A check says the work is done, claimed by whoever did it, worker or you, with a name on it. A Sign Off is yours alone: you looked, it is so. The button sits at the foot of the car's card, Sign Off & Publish: one tap signs every checked task and publishes the car's whole Current Status in the same motion, and the headline writes itself from the work signed. The customer never sees the board; they see the last thing you signed. If a signed task has to reopen, uncheck it: it returns to open work, the record keeps the date it reopened, and your next sign-off shows it to the customer as in progress again. A quick-add at the foot of the board starts a job with just a customer, a car, and a job name, for the moment a car rolls in; the full Commit remains the way for the real intake. LIVE

6. Part purchase. A task needs a part, and there are two ways it gets bought. The first: from the part you tap Ask Customer to Buy and paste the seller's link; add your judgment if you have some. The customer pays the seller directly and has it shipped to the shop; the money never passes through your hands or the app. The customer answers Bought it, Not now, or Call me, and sends a photo of the receipt when they have bought. The second way: the shop buys it. Then the part's record says so, with what the shop paid (sealed, like all money), and the part becomes a line on the job's invoice. A part the customer bought is listed on the record too, marked customer-supplied, and is never a charge.

The part walks its own road: Needed, Asked, Ordered, Arrived; Arrived is marked at the shop, in the part's own window on the board, and every part not yet arrived sits on the pending list, grouped by car. One record, one road, whoever buys: if a part is already on order and somebody records buying it again, from either side, the app does not quietly nod. It flags the duplicate for you to sort out, because two of a part is a real thing that happens in real shops. When you ask a customer to buy, the Ask page lets you pick the part from the car's board list, and the request carries that part on the record; sending marks the part Asked. A raised flag shows three ways: the part's tag on the board carries a warning, a card lands in Questions telling the story, and the part's own window on the board asks the only question that settles it: which purchase stands? Tap Shop's stands and the shop's order is the record, or Customer's stands and the customer's purchase is, never charged to them, while the shop returns its own order. The other purchase gets returned or cancelled out in the world; the app keeps which stood, and who decided, on the part's record. The flag blocks nothing while it stands. Ask flow LIVE Receipt photo LIVE Duplicate flag LIVE

7. Job completion. The last task signs off and the job is done as work. You tap Complete Job. The customer's page closes the job with its date and keeps the whole Story readable; the record goes read-only, and if the customer has a staged car waiting, the app offers to start it. LIVE

8. Invoice prep. The job's accounting, kept in the app from the start, becomes the invoice: labor from the hours record, parts at what the record says, the deposit credited, the balance plain. You review it before it exists for anyone else; nothing reaches the customer that you have not seen. COMING with the accounting build.

9. Final payment. Most days this step and the next are the same moment: the customer arrives with the payment in hand and leaves with the car. The app records the payment against the job as it happens, so the ledger and the drawer agree. COMING with the accounting build.

10. Delivery. The customer collects the car. Delivery is your call, not the balance's: sometimes you release a car on a partial payment and credit, because the workspace is worth more than the wait. The app is built for that. The job closes; the account does not have to. What is still owed becomes the customer's tab, kept on their account across jobs, and later payments knock it down until it reaches zero. Their page keeps the finished job and its Story as their record of the work, and the next job for that car, whenever it comes, opens as a new chapter on the same car, under the same invitation. Nothing about the old job rewrites itself. Job side LIVE Tab COMING

That is the whole road. Every other chapter in this book is one of these steps, opened up.

5. Questions and Answers

The message center: customer questions, Coco's answers, Accept / Add / Correct, answers of record, and the OR function (Omar OR Tío Clave). Photos land here too, and not only receipts. A customer can send a photo with a message, from the picture button in Tell Omar on their page; whatever they typed rides along as the words that go with the picture. A worker can send a photo from his own link, as proof of work or to ask about something he found; his card here says Photo from, and his name. Receipts arrive the same way, from a buy request or from the Receipts section on the car card. Every photo card works the same: open it, look, mark it reviewed, or handle it another way.

And if a photo lands on the wrong car, which happens (a customer taps the wrong card), its card carries Move to: pick the right car, same customer, and the photo and its card move together. A photo never crosses to a different customer. If it was tied to a request on the old car, that tie is cut, and the card tells you it was.

Coco answers customer questions herself now, the moment a question lands. She may only repeat what Omar has signed off, and she says so in the answer: her first answer in a conversation introduces her, later ones run shorter, and they close by saying Omar should confirm. Her answer sits in a green panel on the question's card, with three buttons. Accept says her answer stands as the shop's answer: the question closes, and on the customer's conversation page her answer gains a check mark, Omar agreed. Add lets you type more and Send: your words post after hers, under your name. Correct lets you type the right answer and Send: yours replaces hers on the customer's page, and hers is not shown there again. A plain reply without the buttons still works; her answer simply stays neither agreed nor corrected. Small print under her words names the reader that wrote the answer, and every answer she gives is checked by a second reader before it posts.

Sometimes the green panel is not there, and a short line on the card says why. If the question reaches past the signed-off status (a price, a promise, a date she has not been given), she passes it to you without answering. If the last sign-off is more than two weeks old, she passes too, until the next sign-off freshens it. If her draft fails its fact check, it is never sent, and the card says so. And if a crowd of questions lands in the same minute, she sends a short note saying everybody arrived at once; the question still waits for you.

A duplicate purchase raises its own card here: it tells which part, which car, and who bought what, and it carries no reply box. It is cleared on the board, in the part's window, and when it is cleared there this card clears itself.

12. Shop Tasks

Not every job in a shop belongs to a car. Sweeping the bay, installing the new lift, the work that keeps the place a shop: that is Shop Tasks, and it lives on the board as its own card.

Pick Shop Tasks in the car selector, second from the top, always there. The card looks like a car card because it works like one: the same rows, the same grips. Drag to set the order; the order is the priority, and it is the same order the workers see on their links. The checkbox finishes a task and strikes it through. The ✕ cancels it; a cancelled task leaves the board, and you can bring it back from the full list. The + Task row at the foot adds a task with just its name.

The card's Full list is where the rest lives: give a task its detail and category (General is the everyday bucket), hand it to a worker or leave it unassigned, and see the finished and cancelled ones with who did what, and when. A worker sees the list on his own link and can start and finish the tasks that carry his name; assigning and cancelling are yours.

Shop Tasks never touch a customer's page. There is no customer. LIVE

Book II — Workers

1. Your Day, Start to Finish

Open your link. At the top is Working on: pick the car you are on, or leave it on Shop work, the bucket for work that belongs to no car. The card under it shows that car's status exactly as Omar last signed it off, and under it the job's live Tasks and Parts: the real list from the board, not the signed-off copy. Check a task when you have done it; your name goes on the check. Add a task the job turns out to need; your name goes on that too. When a part lands, tap Arrived on it. Do the work. Write your hours against what you were working on. If something needs saying (you replaced a panel, you found rust, you are waiting on a decision), say it in Tell Omar: it lands on the record with your name on it, and it cannot be edited afterward, so a correction is a second note, not a quiet rewrite. A photo can ride with it: the picture button in the same card sends a photo of the work, with whatever you typed as its caption, and Omar sees it in Questions with your name on it. Photos go with a car, so pick the car first. Shop work that Omar has handed out sits in Shop Tasks, in his order; start yours when you start it, finish it when it is done. LIVE

2. Your Link Is Your Key

One link per worker. It carries your name, and everything you touch is stamped with it. Your hours can only ever be written under your own name; the app makes any other way impossible, not just impolite. Guard the link like a key. LIVE

3. What You Can Do

See every live job's signed-off status. Work the job's live task list: check tasks you have done, add tasks the job turns out to need, and mark parts Arrived when they land, each stamped with your name. Write and correct your own hours, per car or as Shop work, back ninety days. Leave notes for Omar on any job, or on Shop work. Send a photo of your work from Tell Omar, as proof or to ask about what you found. Start and finish Shop Tasks assigned to you. LIVE

4. What You Cannot Do

Sign off, see money, commit, end a job. Not a slight: the signature is Omar's alone, and it is what the customer trusts. LIVE as the standing law.

5. Checking Is a Claim

Your check says “I did this”; Omar's sign-off says “it is so.” Both are on the record, with names. The check lives on the car's card on your page, next to the task it belongs to. LIVE
